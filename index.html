About

I am a Master's student in Computer Vision at Carnegie Mellon University, motivated by curiosity-driven research, a deep love for mathematics, and the desire to solve meaningful problems with real-world impact.

My research interests include diffusion and flow-based generative models, object-centric learning, 3D/4D scene understanding, and world models for embodied AI. I am especially drawn to problems that require both mathematical rigor and creative thinking: understanding objects, motion, geometry, and structure in dynamic scenes; building generative models that capture the underlying dynamics of the world; and developing intelligent systems that reason, adapt, and generalize.

Research Interests

Generative Modeling Diffusion & Flows 3D/4D Vision Object-Centric Learning Spatial Intelligence Embodied AI Autonomous Driving Domain Adaptation

Object-Centric 4D Scene Understanding

Exploring slot-based representations over frozen geometric backbones for dynamic multi-view scenes. The goal is to bridge low-level geometric tracking with high-level object-level understanding.

Diffusion, Flows, and Lifted Representations

Studying generative modeling methods for tilted distributions, lifted feature spaces, reward-conditioned sampling, and structured representations for controllable generation.

Generative AI for Aerial Perception

Using foundation generative models to create controlled synthetic datasets for diagnosing failure modes in aerial-view object detectors.

Autonomous Driving & Intent Reasoning

Prior work on pedestrian intent prediction, visual reasoning, domain-adaptive object detection, and robust perception for autonomous systems.
